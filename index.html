Follow-up Prompt: How might employers evaluate graduates differently if AI tools become standard in university education?
Show 150+ Model Sample (Part 1 & Part 2)

Part 1 (114 words): Universities should integrate AI tools into coursework rather than imposing blanket bans. Admittedly, unrestricted use raises legitimate concerns about plagiarism and shallow critical thinking. Nevertheless, prohibiting a technology that dominates modern workplaces fails to prepare students for professional reality. Specifically, professors can redesign assignments to evaluate source verification, original synthesis, and ethical judgment alongside final prose. For instance, business courses could require students to critique an AI-generated market analysis and justify strategic revisions in class. Consequently, learners gain practical fluency while preserving core analytical rigor. Ultimately, thoughtful integration aligns academic standards with evolving career demands.

Part 2 (71 words): If AI assistance becomes universal, employers will shift away from judging polished take-home essays toward testing spontaneous reasoning. Specifically, recruiters will prioritize live case interviews, oral defenses, and problem-solving simulations where candidates must interpret ambiguous data independently. Moreover, firms will value graduates who demonstrate strong editorial judgment and domain expertise, since spotting subtle algorithmic errors quickly becomes a decisive competitive advantage.
